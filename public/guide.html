Quick start

The program replaces first-come-first-served sign-up. Families rank up to 3 choices per period on a personal web form. After the deadline, a solver assigns every camper to their best available choices without ever going over an activity's capacity. Anyone it can't place is flagged so you can finish their schedule by hand.

Your job in three lines: (1) get the roster in and links out, (2) chase anyone who hasn't submitted, (3) after scheduling, work through the campers who need help.
One thing the dashboard can't do: run the scheduler itself. After the deadline, ask the program director to run it (the whole season takes about a minute). Everything before and after that step is in the dashboard.

Signing in

  1. Open the dashboard link the program director sent you.
  2. Enter the access code (ask the director — it's the same for the whole team). Your browser remembers it, so you'll only type it once per computer.
  3. The camp name shows in the blue header (for example aaron2027). Sign out is at the top right — use it on shared computers.

Never put the access code in an email subject or a public chat. Family links are different — each family only ever sees their own child's form.

The season, step by step

1. Before the form opens

1
Upload the roster. Roster & Links → Upload / replace roster. Use a CSV with the columns camper_id,name,division,grade,gender and, if campers attend only some weeks, weeks (like 1;2;5;8).
  • Divisions: Gilad = 5th grade, Arad = 6th grade, Tavor = 7th–8th grade.
  • gender (M or F) matters — a few activities (Girls Gaga, Girls Basketball, Girls Soccer) are only offered to girls. Leave it blank only if you must.
  • Re-uploading is safe: campers already on the roster keep their same link, so links you already emailed keep working.
2
Try it yourself first. Open the practice camp and fill out a sample form so you know what parents will see.
3
Send each family their link. On Roster & Links, Download all links (CSV) gives you name, division and link for every camper — use it for a mail merge. Or press Copy link beside one camper.

2. While families are filling it in

4
Watch the progress cards at the top of Roster & Links (submitted, still waiting, completion %, per division).
5
Chase the stragglers. Click the Pending filter to see who hasn't submitted, or Copy pending links for a ready-made reminder list.
Families can reopen their link and change their answers any time until you run the scheduler, so a mistake is never a problem.

3. After the deadline

6
Ask the program director to run the scheduler. It solves each camp week separately, using only the campers attending that week.

4. Reviewing the results

7
Campers tab — see who needs help and fix them (see "Needs help").
8
Capacity tab — look for activities under min (too few campers to run) and anything nearly full.
9
Friends tab — check that friend and same-schedule pairs came out the way families expected.
10
Print schedules. Open any camper on the Campers tab and press Print.

What families see

Each family gets a personal link (it contains a private key, so they can only ever see and change their own child's form). On a phone it looks like this:

  • A short "How this works" box that explains the rules below.
  • A Swim Alternative checkbox for Arad and Tavor campers who want to skip instructional swim.
  • One card per period, Monday to Friday. Each card has a required 1st choice and optional 2nd and 3rd backups. The lists only show activities that camper is eligible for (grade and gender).
  • A Friends section: up to two friend requests plus an optional "same schedule as" request. Families pick a name from a list of the campers in their child's division, so names can't be misspelled.
  • A progress bar and Submit button pinned to the bottom. If something is missing or breaks a rule, the form says exactly what and highlights the period in red.
  • After submitting they see a thank-you page with their 1st choices, and can reopen the same link to make changes.
Families often complete the form together with their friends so the choices match — that's exactly what gives the best chance of being scheduled together.

The rules (enforced on the form and by the scheduler)

RuleWhat it means
One activity per periodA camper does exactly one thing in each period. Capacity is never exceeded — if a class is full, the next choice is used.
Once a weekA camper can't take the same activity twice in a week. Exceptions: sports activities (Basketball, Soccer, Kickball, Gaga…) and Lake Inflatables can be taken twice. Swim Alternative campers can take Gaga or 9 Square in the Air three times.
Double periods and all-week activitiesActivities like Coding, Things That Go, Master Chef and Advanced Art fill two periods; Theater fills every day; Sports League, Perform Dance and Advanced Wheel fill specific days. Picking one covers all of those periods, and the form greys the covered periods out.
Can't take bothAdvanced Wheel and Wheel Ceramics can't both be taken, and neither can Master Chef and Top Chef.
Grade and genderCampers only see and get activities they're eligible for.
Friend requestBoth campers must name each other. The scheduler then tries to put them in the same activity when they both ranked it (the reward is biggest when they ranked it the same number). It's a nudge, not a promise.
Same scheduleBoth campers must name each other. Then, in every period, whatever one gets, the other gets the identical thing. If their choices don't overlap in a period, both are left for staff to place. This is the strictest rule.
Never guessIf none of a camper's three choices is available in a period, the scheduler does not drop them into a random class — it flags them for you.

"Needs help" — finishing a schedule by hand

A camper needs help when a period is unfilled: every choice they gave was full, they'd already used it that week, or they're tied to a same-schedule partner whose choices didn't overlap.

How to work through them

  1. Campers tab → pick the week → Load. The counts at the top show how many campers and periods need attention.
  2. Click a camper. Red Needs help squares are their unfilled periods. Below the grid, each unfilled period lists what they asked for and how many seats are open right now.
  3. Press Fix in Reassign. Each unfilled period is a dropdown; choose an activity (the number in brackets is seats open). Save changes.
  4. The camper's grid now shows the new activity tagged assigned by staff. They drop off the needs-help list once every period is filled.

Good to know

  • Double periods and all-week activities move as one. Choosing one fills every period it covers (you'll be asked to confirm if it replaces something). Removing one frees them all. Later periods of an activity show as locked — change it from the first period.
  • Swim Alternative periods are editable like any other.
  • A full activity can't be chosen — the page refuses and tells you.
  • The repeat-limit and can't-take-both rules give a warning you can override if you really mean to.
  • Same-schedule pairs: the Campers tab flags them. To keep the pair identical, make the same change for both kids — Reassign won't stop you from breaking the match.
  • If a family emails to change something after scheduling, you can usually just do it in Reassign.

Common questions

A family lost their link.

Roster & Links → search their name → Copy link, and send it again. It's the same link as before.

A family says their link "isn't valid."

The link is cut off or edited (the long code at the end matters). Copy a fresh one from the dashboard and resend it.

A child is in the wrong division, grade, or missing a week.

Fix the row in your roster CSV and upload it again. Everyone keeps their link; their existing choices are kept too. If the change moves them to a different division, ask them to reopen their link and re-check their choices (each division has its own activities).

A camper was added after the deadline.

Upload the updated roster, send them their link, then ask the director to re-run the scheduler.

Someone wants to see their child's schedule.

Campers tab → their name → Print.

Why is one activity "under min"?

Fewer campers signed up than the minimum needed to run it. Either fill it by moving campers in through Reassign, or move the few who are in it elsewhere.

Two friends aren't together but both wanted the same activity. Why?

Usually the activity filled up, or one of them had already used it that week (activities are once a week). The Friends tab shows exactly which periods they share.

The numbers changed after I edited someone.

That's correct — Reassign updates the seat counts everywhere immediately.

Can two of us use the dashboard at once?

Yes for looking around. If two people edit the same week at the same moment, the last save wins, so coordinate who is fixing which campers.

Swim Alternative capacity?

There are no seat limits on Swim Alternative activities yet (the source data has no numbers for them), so the scheduler treats them as unlimited.

Closing a small class

  1. Capacity tab → pick the week → tick under minimum only to see the small classes.
  2. Press Close class…. A preview lists every enrolled camper and where they would go — always one of their own other choices, never something they didn't ask for.
  3. Campers with no open backup are shown as Needs help; finish them in Reassign. Same-schedule pairs move together or are left for you.
  4. Press Close class and move campers. The class shows CLOSED, has zero seats, and can't be picked in Reassign. Reopen undoes the closure (campers already moved stay put).

Closures are kept if the scheduler is re-run, but a re-run replaces any manual moves you made in Reassign.
